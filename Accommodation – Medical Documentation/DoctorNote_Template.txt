Physician's Note — Workplace Accommodation (OHRC)
Ready-to-sign version for Dr. K. Sotoudeh — Bradford Health Care Centre.

What this note must do (so the doctor doesn't need to know OHRC):
  1. Confirm a disability exists.
  2. State the functional limitations (commuting = trigger; plus anxiety /
     trauma-related limitations as confirmed).
  3. State the accommodation needed (remote work, as functional limitations require).
  4. State duration (ongoing, with reassessment).
Avoid: treatment details, medication, history, diagnosis — HR only needs
functional information. This is the OHRC chain: disability → limitations →
accommodation → duration. (The employer may generally only ask: does a
disability exist; what are the limitations; can essential duties be performed
with/without accommodation; what accommodation is needed — and must pay for
required medical documentation.)

Multiple conditions: the note deliberately does not name conditions — it
covers the COMBINED functional impact of all confirmed conditions (substance
use, anxiety, trauma-related). The bracketed bullets below leave room for
Peter to add the social anxiety and possible PTSD limitations.

Peter's report will recommend this note; Dr. Sotoudeh adapts and signs it.

Who states vs. who chooses the accommodation (OHRC):
- The note SHOULD state the accommodation NEED — OHRC Policy on ableism (s. 8.7)
  expects medical documentation to include "the type of accommodation(s) that
  may be needed." Stating remote work is correct, not overreach.
- The employer IMPLEMENTS the accommodation and, where two options meet the
  needs equally, may choose the less disruptive one (s. 8.3: the Code
  accommodates NEEDS, not PREFERENCES).
- Here the limitation IS the commute, so remote work is the accommodation that
  meets the need — worded below as "required" (need), not "preferred."

Self-contained note — no reference to other providers (OHRC ss. 8.7, 8.8):
- The note stands alone. Do NOT mention the psychologist, the assessment, or
  any other provider. The employer "does not have the right to know a person's
  confidential medical information" (s. 8.7), and documentation "should be
  provided only to the people who need to be aware of the information" (s. 8.8).
- A treating physician may rely on specialist material without disclosing it;
  the note says only "based on my clinical assessment and ongoing care of this
  patient" — true, and sufficient. It must not claim a psychological
  assessment was performed.

[CLINIC LETTERHEAD]

Date: ____________________

To Whom It May Concern (Attention: Human Resources):

Re: Ken Wood — DOB: ____________________

I am the treating physician for Mr. Ken Wood.

This note is based on my clinical assessment and ongoing care of this patient.

Mr. Wood has diagnosed medical conditions that constitute a disability within
the meaning of the Ontario Human Rights Code.

Mr. Wood experiences ongoing functional limitations that are triggered and
worsened by commuting to and from the workplace:
- Commuting is a trigger that worsens his conditions and increases the risk
  of relapse.
- Driving causes significant anticipatory anxiety and a heightened stress
  response that persists after arrival and for one to two days afterward.
- Sleep is disrupted on nights before in-office attendance, reducing his
  ability to maintain stability.
- Commuting-related fatigue reduces concentration, consistency, and the
  ability to maintain the routines that support his health.
- These limitations fluctuate; their impact is greater during periods of
  elevated symptoms.

[Additional limitations — for the psychologist to confirm and add:]
- Anticipatory anxiety and avoidance related to driving and the commute.
- Heightened anxiety and a startle response to traffic events that evoke
  past traumatic incidents.
- Intrusive, trauma-related thoughts and hypervigilance while driving.

Accommodation required:
Mr. Wood requires a workplace accommodation permitting him to work from home
as his functional limitations require, including full-time remote work during
periods of elevated symptoms. In-office attendance should be limited to the
extent necessary to avoid triggering or worsening these limitations.

[Alternative wording, only if a fixed limit is required:]
Mr. Wood should be restricted to working in the office no more than __ days
per week, with the balance of his work performed remotely.

Duration: Ongoing, with reassessment after 12 months.

Please contact my office if further information is required.

Signed: ______________________________   Date: ______________
[Physician name, credentials, CPSO #]
